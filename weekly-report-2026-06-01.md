### STORY 1: GitHub Copilot Ends Flat-Rate Pricing — Usage-Based Billing Starts Today

**Source:** GitHub Blog, GitHub Docs, Visual Studio Magazine, Windows Forum
**Date:** June 1, 2026 (effective today)

**Summary:**
GitHub Copilot has replaced flat-rate subscription pricing with usage-based billing across all plans, effective June 1, 2026. Every plan now includes a monthly allotment of GitHub AI Credits — with one AI credit equalling $0.01 USD — and usage is calculated based on token consumption across input, output, and cached tokens. Code completions and next-edit suggestions remain unlimited on paid plans and are not charged against credits. Plan pricing is: Copilot Pro+ at $39/month (includes $39 in credits), Copilot Business at $19/user/month (includes $19 in credits), and Copilot Enterprise at $39/user/month (includes $39 in credits). For June, July, and August, existing Business and Enterprise customers receive promotional included usage to ease the transition. Developer community reaction has been mixed — with some on Visual Studio Magazine's forums noting bluntly: "You will get less, but pay the same price" — particularly for power users of premium models like Claude and GPT-5. Credits can now be pooled across organisations rather than siloing unused usage per user, which benefits larger teams. The change marks the end of the "all-you-can-eat" era for AI developer tools.

**Why it matters to SMEs:**
SMEs using GitHub Copilot — particularly software development firms and tech-forward businesses — now need to actively monitor AI usage costs rather than treating them as a fixed overhead. The shift rewards intentional, efficient AI use and penalises indiscriminate prompting. Finance teams will need new processes to track and cap spend.

**Why it matters to Tulivo Digital:**
This is a template for how all AI tooling pricing will evolve. Tulivo Digital can build a "AI Spend Optimisation" advisory service — helping SME clients audit their AI consumption, identify waste, and architect workflows that minimise token costs while maximising output. This is a highly repeatable, defensible service as more platforms follow suit.

### STORY 6: ICO Closes Consultation on AI Recruitment — Enforcement Phase Begins

**Source:** ICO.org.uk, DLA Piper Privacy Matters, Farrer & Co, Keystone Law
**Date:** Consultation closed May 29, 2026

**Summary:**
The UK Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) closed its consultation on automated decision-making (ADM) in recruitment on May 29, 2026. The consultation followed an ICO report published in March 2026 that reviewed evidence from more than 30 UK employers, finding that most organisations using AI to screen and score candidates are constituting automated decision-making under UK data protection law — without applying the required safeguards. The ICO found that most employers "considered it unlikely" that they were using ADM, when in practice they were. Direct letters have been sent to 16 named organisations. The ICO's position: employers must inform candidates that automated decisions are being made, clearly explain how AI tools function, and ensure meaningful human involvement before shortlisting decisions take effect. Separately, the House of Commons Business and Trade Committee has launched an inquiry into AI in the UK workplace, examining whether existing worker protections remain fit for purpose. Over 70% of organisations plan to increase AI use in recruitment over the next five years, making this a rapidly growing compliance risk.

**Why it matters to SMEs:**
Any UK SME using AI to sort CVs, score candidates, or shortlist applicants is likely non-compliant under current ICO guidance. With enforcement now following consultation, fines and reputational damage are real risks — particularly as the ICO has already named organisations.

**Why it matters to Tulivo Digital:**
This is an immediate sales opportunity. Tulivo can position itself as the partner that helps SMEs "get AI hiring right" — auditing current tools, redesigning workflows to include mandatory human checkpoints, and updating candidate communication. This is a compliance-driven service that doesn't require SMEs to be enthusiastic about AI — it just requires them to not want an ICO letter.
